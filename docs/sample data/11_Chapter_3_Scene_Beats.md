# CHAPTER 3: MOTHER'S SECRETS
## Scene Beats

Written in the style of Daphne du Maurier, emphasizing social hierarchy and hidden powers.

---

## Scene 1: Morning After (500 words)

**Setting:** Émilie's bedroom, early morning

- Émilie haunted by the creature revealed in Charles's pistol flash
- Her sketches of the entity keep distorting—angles bending impossibly, lines twisting away from what she remembers
- Each attempt to capture the creature's form makes her head ache
- Crumpled papers show fragments: too many joints, movements that shouldn't be possible, darkness that seemed to absorb the pistol's light
- Mother enters with breakfast tray, carrying herself with practiced noble bearing
- Tension as mother notices the sketches, quickly gathering them up
- Strange occurrence: tea leaves form shapes that make mother quickly clear away the cups
- Mother watching Émilie's reactions with mixture of calculation and concern
- Scene ends with Émilie demanding to know what Charles shot, why she can't draw it

---

## Scene 2: Noble Blood (600 words)

**Setting:** Mother's private sitting room

- Mother locking door, checking mirrors with practiced ritual gestures
- Explanation of their noble lineage through her father
- Mother demonstrates her acknowledged but "weakened" noble magic
- Discussion of how society views mixed blood as diluting noble power
- Careful explanation of salon's official role in noble magical hierarchy
- Mother's bitter comments about nobles who consider her magic lesser
- Émilie learning about rigid structure of blood magic system
- Undercurrent of unspoken observations about other, unexplained phenomena

---

## Scene 3: The City's Change (500 words)

**Setting:** Salon balcony overlooking street

- Mother and Émilie watching crowd below
- Mother explains how her "weakened" noble magic helps her sense mob energy
- Street vendor selling revolutionary pamphlets
- Sudden manifestation of crowd's power—stones throwing themselves
- Mother's official explanation interrupted by unexpected magical effects around them
- Charles appearing below, treating mother with careful noble courtesy
- Signs of supernatural changes in city architecture
- Discussion of how noble magic is failing against revolutionary forces

---

## Scene 4: Careful Words (400 words)

**Setting:** Salon's main room, afternoon

- Charles paying official visit
- Mother maintaining perfect noble etiquette while directing conversation
- Discussion of magical politics carefully avoiding certain topics
- Charles respectfully acknowledging mother's noble magic while noting "unusual occurrences"
- Growing attraction between Charles and Émilie complicated by class awareness
- Supernatural intrusion: mirror showing glimpse of bound entity
- Charles's traditional magical response contrasting with Émilie's instinctive reaction
- Mother smoothly explaining away the discrepancy

---

## Scene 5: Unspoken Powers (500 words)

**Setting:** Mother's ritual room

- Mother leading Émilie to hidden chamber, explaining her official role in noble magic
- Demonstration of traditional seal maintenance rituals
- Mother guides Émilie through basic noble magic, surprised when Émilie shows unexpected aptitude
- Discussion of how last night's injured noble would normally be their strongest ritualist
- Mother explains noble was distracted by ritual requirements when creature attacked
- Émilie performing noble magical exercises far beyond what her "diluted" blood should allow
- Mother's careful observation of Émilie's unexpected strength with traditional magic
- Theoretical discussion of blood ratios and power that doesn't explain Émilie's abilities
- **Chapter climax:** Mob energy disrupting salon's protective wards
- **Final beat:** Mother directing Émilie through noble magical defense, both pretending not to notice how much power Émilie channels

## Key Themes

The chapter emphasizes the distinction between acknowledged noble magic (even if considered weakened by mixed blood) and unexplained mixed blood abilities. It establishes that mixed blood allows for unexpected strength in noble magic rather than interference, while maintaining the incomprehensible nature of the bound entities and the rigid social/magical hierarchy of pre-revolutionary Paris.
