# Chapter 2: Through the Glass

## Scene 1: The Waiting

The rough stone pressed against Émilie's shoulder as she crouched in the hidden passage, eye fixed to the peephole that revealed her mother's salon beyond. Not the glittering public face of it—rouge-cheeked nobles laughing over cards and wine—but its secret heart, laid bare through this fish-eye glimpse like a stage viewed through the wrong end of a telescope.

The passage itself was hardly wider than her shoulders, the walls radiating a damp chill that seemed to seep through her silk dress. Cobwebs brushed her face, their touch so light she might have imagined it, save for the way they clung to her skin with ghostly persistence. The air hung thick with age and secrets, musty like old books left too long in a forgotten library.

Through the peephole, she watched her mother direct the evening's choreography with familiar grace. "My dear Comte de Vaudémont," she murmured, touching his sleeve with practiced delicacy, "do let me show you to a most agreeable spot. The evening light is particularly kind here." Her voice carried clearly through the wall, each word as precise as a chess move. The comte followed her gesture with the unquestioning acceptance of long habit, even as his shoulders tensed at the sound of distant shouting from the street—something about bread prices and justice.

One by one, she positioned them—twelve nobles in total, each guided to their places with suggestions so subtle they seemed to choose them themselves. A touch here, a smile there, a delicate suggestion about the quality of the brandies served at certain tables. Only now, watching from her hidden vantage, did Émilie see the pattern emerging. Each carefully placed guest formed part of a larger design, their arrangement echoing the same symbols she'd glimpsed carved into the floor beneath the carpets.

A cold draft whispered past her ear, though the passage had no windows, no source for such a breeze. Near the salon's largest mirror, the shadows seemed to pool and gather like spilled ink, darker than they should be in the well-lit room. She pressed closer to the peephole, trying to better see this strange effect, but the chill only intensified, raising gooseflesh along her arms.

Her mother's voice continued its gentle orchestration, but underneath Émilie caught a new note—tension, perhaps, or fear—as she guided the last pieces into position. The clamor from outside grew louder, carrying fragments about "noble parasites" and "people's justice." Her mother's smile never wavered. "My dear Marquis," she said, moving smoothly between him and the nearest mirror, "I've just had our cellarer bring up a particularly fine Bordeaux that I think you'll find most intriguing. Might I suggest—" She paused, head tilting slightly as though listening to something Émilie couldn't hear. "Ah yes, I believe you'll find the corner setting allows you to best appreciate its qualities."

Émilie watched the marquis settle into his assigned place, noteworthy in how deliberately he arranged himself to avoid catching his reflection in any of the salon's many mirrors. Around him, other nobles made similar subtle adjustments—a chair angled just so, a glance quickly averted from a too-dark reflection. Their quiet conversations about the Constitution seemed forced, artificial against the raw anger echoing from the streets.

The cold spot near her shoulder deepened, and for a moment she could have sworn she saw movement in the narrow passage behind her, a suggestion of something that pulled back into darkness when she turned her head. But that was impossible. She was alone in this cramped space, watching a simple gambling salon prepare for evening business.

Wasn't she?

## Scene 2: The Gathering

Émilie held her breath as her mother moved through the salon's final transformation. The elderly butler Girard, who had served their family since before she was born, moved with practiced efficiency to lock each door. The heavy click of each bolt falling into place echoed with dreadful finality through both the salon and Émilie's hidden passage.

The Marquis de Lafayette emerged from the shadows near the largest mirror, his military bearing unmistakable even as he shed his elaborate coat. "Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe," he said, voice tight with controlled urgency, "the private salon—"

"Is prepared for tomorrow night's ritual, yes," her mother interrupted, already helping nobles retrieve their robes from hiding places. "But the barrier here is failing now. I felt it during play this evening. We cannot wait."

Dark robes emerged from beneath loose floorboards and behind false panels in the wood trim. The rich silk and velvet of court dress disappeared beneath heavy fabric the color of old blood, embroidered with symbols that made Émilie's eyes hurt if she looked at them too directly.

"I sensed it too," Lafayette agreed grimly. "The shadows move too freely in these mirrors. If we wait until tomorrow's appointed time..." He left the thought unfinished.

Charles de Sartine moved at Lafayette's direction, guiding the others to help shift the heavy gaming tables aside. The carpet beneath, when rolled back, revealed more of the strange symbols she'd glimpsed earlier—intricate patterns carved into the floorboards and inlaid with what looked like silver but somehow absorbed rather than reflected the candlelight.

"The barriers grow weaker," the Comtesse de Maillé murmured, her face startlingly severe without her towering wig and rouge. "The rabble in the streets—their anger feeds what lies beyond. And now this breach..."

"This location will serve," Émilie's mother said, moving with swift precision to arrange the circle. "The seals here are strong, if strained. Better to act now than risk waiting."

Through the windows, the last light of day had faded, leaving the salon lit only by candles that seemed to burn with an unnatural steadiness. The mirrors that lined the walls looked wrong in this light—deeper somehow, as though they opened onto spaces that should not exist. Émilie caught movement in their depths, too far back to be mere reflections.

The cold spot behind her had grown, spreading until her whole body trembled with it. The darkness at the far end of the passage seemed to have thickened, becoming almost solid. Something stirred in that darkness, something that made her want to run but held her frozen in place.

"They're pressing closer," Lafayette warned, drawing his silver dagger. "I can feel them testing the edges."

In the salon beyond the peephole, the twelve nobles arranged themselves in a precise circle around the revealed floor patterns. Charles stood directly before the largest mirror, his shoulders set with grim determination. One by one, at Lafayette's nod, they drew their own daggers from the folds of their robes.

The darkness pressed closer. In the mirrors, shadows moved like living things, testing their bonds. And somewhere in the depths of the hidden passage, something answered.

## Scene 3: The Ritual Begins

The transformation of the gambling salon to ritual chamber completed itself with terrible swiftness. Lafayette raised his dagger, silver blade catching candlelight, and began to chant in archaic French that made Émilie's ears ring strangely. The other nobles joined in, their voices weaving together in patterns that seemed to pull at the very air.

One by one, they drew their blades across their palms. The blood fell in precise drops onto the silver-inlaid patterns in the floor. Émilie watched, transfixed, as each crimson drop struck the metal and began to move with unnatural purpose, traveling along the intricate designs as though pulled by an invisible current. The silver seemed to drink in the blood, growing darker with each absorbed drop.

Her mother's voice rose above the others, directing the chant with the same authoritative grace she used to conduct her salon. But there was something different in her tone now—an edge of strain Émilie had never heard before.

"Hold the circle," Lafayette commanded as the largest mirror began to ripple like disturbed water. "They sense our presence now."

The nobles tightened their formation, blood still dripping from their cupped palms. Each drop raced along the silver lines with increasing speed, as though the patterns themselves had grown hungry. Charles stood perfectly still before the rippling mirror, his reflection distorting in ways that made Émilie's stomach lurch. Behind him, something moved in the glass—a darkness deeper than shadow, a presence that bent the candlelight around it.

"The eastern seal first," her mother called out, her voice steady despite the sweat beading on her forehead. "Before they can—"

A sound cut through the chanting—part human scream, part metallic shriek, like a thousand knives being drawn across steel. The candles guttered as the shadows leaped, and the temperature in Émilie's hiding place plummeted until her breath frosted in the air. Through the peephole, she watched in horror as the crack in the largest mirror—the same one she'd seen form during last night's attack—began to widen. The split in the glass spread like ice breaking over a pond, silver surface fracturing with a sound that set her teeth on edge.

Lafayette's chanting took on a desperate intensity. Blood flowed more freely now as the nobles squeezed their wounded palms, feeding the patterns that drank ever more greedily, their silver lines now nearly black with absorbed vitality. The air grew thick with the copper smell of blood and something else—something older and fouler that made Émilie's head spin.

Through the widening crack, shapes began to form—suggestions of limbs and faces that should not exist, that the human mind rejected even as the eye perceived them. They pressed against the remaining glass like creatures trapped beneath ice, testing its strength, searching for weaknesses.

The chanting reached a fever pitch. In the salon, the nobles' voices cracked with strain as they fought to maintain the ritual. But something was changing in the mirror before Charles—the surface no longer merely rippled but began to tear, like fabric ripping along an invisible seam.

In that tear, Émilie caught her first true glimpse of what lay beyond.

## Scene 4: The Breach

In the tear, darkness moved with purpose. Not the simple absence of light, but something that devoured it—a void that twisted and churned like living ink. Through her peephole, Émilie watched as tendrils of this impossible darkness began to seep through the crack, leaving trails of frost in their wake.

"The seal is failing!" Charles's voice carried an edge of panic as he stepped back from the mirror, silver dagger raised. The crack widened further, spreading across the mirror's surface in a web of fractures that sang with that same terrible metallic scream.

"Hold position!" Lafayette commanded. Fresh blood flowed from his palm onto the patterns, racing along the silver lines with desperate speed. "Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe, the eastern point—"

Her mother was already moving, voice rising in a cadence that made Émilie's skull ache. The other nobles followed, their chanting building to a crescendo as more darkness poured through the widening tear. The air grew thick with frost, each breath visible in the rapidly cooling room.

A tendril of darkness lashed out suddenly, wrapping around Charles's raised arm. He cried out—not in pain, but in a horror so profound it made Émilie's heart stutter. Where the darkness touched him, his flesh grew pale, bloodless, as though the very life was being pulled from his skin.

"Cut it!" Lafayette slashed his dagger through the tendril. Where silver met shadow, there was a sound like sizzling ice. The darkness recoiled as Charles fell to the floor, but more was already pouring through the breach.

The nobles tightened their circle, blood flowing freely now onto the patterns. The silver lines drank it in greedily, growing darker and darker until they looked like veins of obsidian laid into the floor. But still the crack spread, still the darkness poured through.

Through her peephole, Émilie could see shapes forming in that darkness—suggestions of limbs, of faces, of things that should not exist in any sane world. They moved with terrible purpose, testing the boundaries of their prison, searching for weakness. And as they moved, they seemed to pull the very warmth from the air itself.

"The pattern!" her mother shouted. "Complete the—"

But before she could finish, the darkness surged through the mirror like a wave of black water. It coalesced into something massive and wrong—a form that seemed to absorb rather than reflect the candlelight, with too many limbs moving in impossible ways. In one fluid motion, it struck the Comtesse de Maillé, sending her flying across the salon to crash against the far wall.

## Scene 5: Discovery

Lafayette moved before the comtesse struck the wall, silver dagger flashing as he spun to face the creature. "Charles! Your pistol!"

Charles scrambled to your feet, fumbling in his coat pocket. Émilie watched through the peephole as he withdrew a small silver pistol, its barrel etched with the same strange symbols she'd seen in the floor patterns. Your creature turned, its form rippling like smoke in a wind that didn't exist.

"It sees—" her mother began, but the thing was already moving, faster than anything that size should be able to move. Charles raised the pistol and fired. Your shot was oddly muffled, as though the air itself swallowed the sound, and in the flash of the discharge, she saw the creature fully for the first time.

Her mind recoiled from what she saw—angles that shouldn't exist, movement that hurt her eyes to follow, a darkness that seemed to devour the very air around it. Where the silver bullet struck, the creature's form collapsed inward like water down a drain, the wound spreading in ripples of nothingness.

Your thing made a sound then—not quite a scream, not quite the shattering of glass, but something that existed in the awful space between the two. It recoiled toward the mirror's surface, its substance drawn into a void of its own making.

"Now!" Lafayette's voice cut through the creature's cry. "Complete the pattern!"

Your nobles pressed forward, their chanting never faltering. Blood flowed from their palms onto the silver lines as the creature began to collapse inward from the bullet wound, its substance spiraling into the void like water down an invisible drain. The very air seemed to bend around the point of its unraveling.

A whisper of movement behind her made Émilie turn. In the darkness of the passage, something waited—patient, ancient, aware. It moved with deliberate slowness, like a predator that knows its prey is cornered. She tried to back away, but her foot caught on an uneven stone. As she fell, her hand struck a jagged edge in the wall, pain flaring sharp and immediate. The smell of her own blood filled the narrow space.

In the salon beyond the peephole, the creature's substance began to unravel like smoke in a sudden wind. Trapped between mirror and void, its form twisted in ways that defied mortal sight before collapsing into nothingness.

The darkness surged forward. Émilie threw up her bleeding hand in a futile attempt to protect herself. Something inside her responded—instinct older than thought—and her blood seemed to catch fire in the air. The creature recoiled with a sound like shattering ice, darkness fragmenting before the unexpected power in her veins.

The blood-darkened patterns on the floor pulsed one final time before fading back to ordinary silver, the ritual complete. Two nobles helped the Comtesse de Maillé to her feet, her face pale but composed despite the violence of her fall. Around them, the air grew warmer as the supernatural cold dissipated.

The mirrors shimmered like disturbed water settling into stillness. Even the crack in the largest glass began to seal itself, edges drawing together until only the faintest line remained to mark where the barrier had nearly broken. The salon's reflections showed nothing now but candlelight and exhausted nobles in their dark robes.

"Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe," Lafayette said, his voice tight with controlled urgency, "your daughter watches us from the walls."

In that moment, as every noble turned toward her hiding place, Émilie realized with perfect clarity that nothing in her life had ever been as it seemed.
