# CHAPTER 1: THE SALON'S SHADOW
## Scene Beats

Written in the style of Anne Rice, emphasizing rich sensory details and psychological horror through aristocratic settings.

---

## Scene 1: Afternoon Preparation (400 words)

**Setting:** Palais-Royal gambling salon, late afternoon

- Sunlight streaming through tall windows creates elongated shadows across polished floors
- Émilie arranging fresh white roses in crystal vases before gilt-framed mirrors
- First supernatural hint: reflection of flowers shifts slightly when Émilie looks away
- Mother, Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe, directing servants with unusual precision about noble seating arrangements
- Specific details about which gaming tables must be placed where, which cards must be used
- Servants polishing silver, cleaning mirrors and crystal - emphasis on reflective surfaces
- Émilie catches glimpse of movement in mirror - a shadow with no source - but dismisses it
- Growing sense of preparation for something beyond simple gambling

---

## Scene 2: Early Evening Arrivals (500 words)

**Setting:** Salon main room, sunset

- Sunset creating strange light patterns through windows, making mirrors seem deeper than they should be
- First nobles arriving in elaborate court dress, jewelry catching and reflecting light oddly
- Introduction of Charles de Sartine - young noble wearing dark blue coat, silver buttons
- Initial attraction/tension as Charles helps Émilie adjust a mirror that won't stay straight
- Mother's careful orchestration of seating - certain nobles must face mirrors, others must not
- Émilie noticing how some nobles avoid looking at their reflections entirely
- Subtle hints of political tension through careful conversations about recent events
- Growing sense of wrongness as mirrors seem to hold reflections too long

---

## Scene 3: The Hidden Door (400 words)

**Setting:** Salon cellar, early evening

- Émilie retrieving more wine from cellar, careful steps down narrow stone stairs
- Noticing cold draft from seemingly solid wall while selecting bottles
- Discovery of slight groove in stone, following it to find hidden catch mechanism
- First view of peephole behind sliding panel, perfectly positioned behind main salon mirror
- Glimpse of hidden chamber beyond gaming room - ritual implements barely visible
- Sound of approaching footsteps forcing quick retreat
- Heart pounding as she returns upstairs, trying to act normal

---

## Scene 4: Evening's Height (400 words)

**Setting:** Main salon, night

- Salon now full of aristocrats, cards snapping, coins clinking
- Growing sense of wrongness in mirrors as night deepens
- Shadows in mirror corners seeming to move independently of light sources
- Charles attempting to warn Émilie through coded conversation about "family traditions"
- Mother's increasing tension visible only to Émilie - subtle signs in her posture and voice
- Certain nobles checking timepieces with increasing frequency
- Servants being dismissed early - unusual for a busy night

---

## Scene 5: The Night Watch (600 words)

**Setting:** Hidden passage, late night

- Most guests departed, specific nobles remaining behind
- Émilie pretending to retire but sneaking back to peephole
- First glimpses of ritual preparation - silver bowls, ancient books
- **Chapter climax:** Creature in passage attacks ritual participants and one is wounded before they kill it
- **Final beat:** Ritual participants finish binding ritual before more monsters emerge

---

## Integration Elements

### Historical Accuracy:
- Detailed descriptions of period clothing and salon customs
- Political conversations reflecting 1791 tensions
- Accurate architectural details of Palais-Royal

### Class Dynamics:
- Servant/noble interactions showing social hierarchy
- Mother's careful navigation of class boundaries
- Émilie's unique position as salon daughter

### Supernatural Build-up:
- Progressive escalation of mirror anomalies
- Growing wrong-ness in reflective surfaces
- Shadows gathering presence throughout evening

### Character Relationships:
- Mother/daughter dynamic through subtle interactions
- Initial attraction between Émilie and Charles
- Servant loyalty and fear shown through small moments

### Revolutionary Context:
- Overheard conversations about political tension
- Nobility's growing unease
- Hints of changing power dynamics in Paris

---

## Tone & Style

Maintains Anne Rice's gothic sensibility while incorporating historically accurate details and building supernatural dread through accumulation of small details rather than overt horror.
