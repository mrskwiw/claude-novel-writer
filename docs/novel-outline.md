# THE BOUND ONES — Master Novel Outline
## v1.0

50,000-word target | 4 Acts | 24 Chapters | Gothic Historical Horror
Setting: Paris, 1791–1794 | Narrator: Marie Tussaud (retrospective)

---

## ACT 1: DISCOVERY (1791)
*~12,500 words | Chapters 1–6*
*Stylistic model: Anne Rice (Ch1), Shirley Jackson (Ch2), Daphne du Maurier (Ch3–6)*

**Arc:** Émilie moves from protected observer to conscious participant who cannot un-know what she knows.
**Supernatural escalation:** Mirror anomalies → peephole witness → entity physical manifestation in ritual → mob energy disrupting wards → entity breaks through in public.

---

### Chapter 1: The Salon's Shadow
**Status:** PROSE COMPLETE
**Date:** November 1791 | **Location:** Salon de Sainte-Amaranthe

Émilie as competent salon daughter, carefully observant. First mirror wrongness. First glimpse of hidden purpose beneath the gambling. Climax: discovery of the hidden passage behind the mirrors.

---

### Chapter 2: Through the Glass
**Status:** PROSE COMPLETE
**Date:** November 1791, same night | **Location:** Hidden passages, ritual chamber

Émilie watches the ritual through the peephole. Lafayette confirms the barrier is failing. Emergency ritual; entity breaks through; Charles disperses it with his silver pistol. Entity notices Émilie. Her blood activates when it approaches in the passage — first instinctive power, alarming not triumphant. The mark is placed (significance withheld until Act 4).

---

### Chapter 3: Mother's Secrets
**Status:** BEATS EXIST — needs prose
**Date:** November 1791, morning after | **Location:** Salon, bedrooms, balcony
**Style:** Daphne du Maurier — social hierarchy, subtext-heavy dialogue, hidden powers beneath proper surfaces

**Scenes:**
- S1 (Morning After): Émilie's bedroom — failed sketches of the entity; lines refuse to hold its form; mother enters with breakfast tray, notices the papers, gathers them
- S2 (Noble Blood): Mother's private sitting room — explanation of their heritage, demonstration of her acknowledged but "weakened" noble magic; the rigid blood-magic hierarchy and her position within it
- S3 (The City's Change): Salon balcony — watching the crowd below; first visible revolutionary magic (stones moving without hands); mother's official explanation fails in real time
- S4 (Careful Words): Charles pays a formal afternoon visit; coded conversation about "certain obligations" while the mirror shows a glimpse of entity; mother smoothly explains it away
- S5 (Unspoken Powers): Mother's ritual room — seal maintenance demonstration; Émilie performs noble exercises far beyond what her "diluted" blood should allow; **Climax:** mob energy from the streets disrupts the salon's protective wards; both women pretending not to notice how much power Émilie channels in the defense

---

### Chapter 4: Wax and Shadows
**Status:** OUTLINE — needs beats + prose
**Date:** December 1791 | **Location:** Tussaud's wax studio, rue Saint-Honoré
**Style:** Daphne du Maurier continuing

**Scenes:**
- S1: Émilie and her mother travel to Tussaud's studio for a portrait commission — a cover for Madame to consult with Tussaud privately
- S2: Portrait sitting; Tussaud's supernatural sensitivity revealed through her observations of Émilie; the wax itself responds strangely
- S3: Street riot visible from the studio window — collective magic moving through the crowd; stones and objects, crowd coordination with physical weight
- S4: Tussaud explains what she has been witnessing — the plebeian side of magic; she has no framework for it but she has been recording it; she is the only person who teaches rather than conceals
- S5: **Climax:** Émilie's touch accidentally activates the half-finished wax portrait — her mixed blood interacting with the wax's supernatural memory; Tussaud and Madame both see it; different reactions; Tussaud begins her chronicle that night

---

### Chapter 5: Breaking Points
**Status:** OUTLINE — needs beats + prose
**Date:** January 1792 | **Location:** Salon de Sainte-Amaranthe, street level
**Style:** Gothic pressure; claustrophobia building

**Scenes:**
- S1: Major ritual preparation — noble families gathering; Émilie sees how many fewer practitioners there are than there should be; early conversation establishes the practitioner count
- S2: Revolutionary energy now visibly affecting the salon's mirrors — not just mirror anomalies but active distortion during the ritual preparation
- S3: Charles warns Émilie privately about growing political dangers specifically aimed at salon gatherings; the political investigation and the supernatural crisis are converging
- S4: Tussaud arrives unexpectedly — she has been chronicling supernatural events in the streets and needs to confer with Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe
- S5: **Climax:** During the ritual, an entity breaks through the salon mirror for the first time in physical manifestation; the Comtesse de Maillé is injured; multiple practitioners required to force it back; this is the first time the salon itself has been breached

---

### Chapter 6: The New Order
**Status:** OUTLINE — needs beats + prose
**Date:** February 1792 | **Location:** Salon, streets
**Style:** Du Maurier; the propriety cracking

**Scenes:**
- S1: Aftermath of the breach; the Comtesse de Maillé recovering; Madame reveals the full truth to Émilie — the seals, the Bound Ones, what they are, what they want, how long this has been managed
- S2: Émilie processes: she is not a dilution of noble power; she is something the system has not seen before
- S3: Revolutionary leaders now investigating supernatural disturbances; their investigation frame (conspiracy/foreign agents) collides with the real phenomenon
- S4: Charles, formally and carefully, offers protection through marriage — not as romantic declaration alone but as practical magical alliance; Émilie's mother is in the room; this is negotiated in the presence of an observer
- S5: **Climax:** Saint-Just appears in the salon for the first time — he is investigating, he senses something he cannot categorize, he leaves having learned nothing definitive but everything suspicious

## ACT 2: TRANSFORMATION (1791–September 1792)
*~12,500 words | Chapters 7–12*
*Stylistic model: Anne Rice — sensory richness, interior interiority, beauty and dread coexisting*

**Arc:** Émilie grows into conscious participant and then into something new — the bridge. Political catastrophe accelerates supernatural collapse.
**Historical anchors:** Monarchy falls August 10, 1792. September Massacres, September 2–7, 1792.

---

### Chapter 7: The Shape of Choice
**Date:** Spring 1792 | **Location:** Salon, private rooms
- Émilie's actual answer to Charles's proposal — given privately, on her terms; her mother is not present for the decision
- Negotiations of what the marriage means for the magical alliance — Charles's family, their seal locations, their obligations
- First moment Émilie consciously directs her power, even slightly — not deliberately activating it but for the first time not being entirely surprised by it
- Tussaud's chronicle of the salon's changing character — fewer nobles, more political figures; the map of the magical network is visibly shrinking

### Chapter 8: What the Crowd Knows
**Date:** Summer 1792 | **Location:** Tussaud's studio, street level
- Tussaud teaching Émilie about what she has been witnessing — plebeian magic; Émilie is the first noble's daughter she has taught; Tussaud teaches without agenda, which is why Émilie trusts her
- Second portrait sitting — Émilie studying herself being studied
- Street crowds during a revolutionary rally; the collective magic visible and enormous; Émilie experiencing it from outside the crowd and feeling it differently than Tussaud does
- First time Émilie consciously tries to understand both systems simultaneously; the synthesis remains just out of reach

### Chapter 9: The Mirror World
**Date:** July–August 1792 | **Location:** Various Paris locations; the salon
- Noble families across Paris reporting seal failures to Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe; she is functioning as a crisis coordinator
- Mirrors in the salon now showing the spaces beyond them more frequently; the thin places are multiplying
- First public supernatural incident — a mirror in a café shatters and what comes through is visible to ordinary people; explained away officially as hysteria; Tussaud documents it
- Entity manifesting in the salon simultaneously; Émilie present; her instinctive response — for the first time the entity retreats from her specifically, not from the ritual circle

### Chapter 10: August
**Date:** August 10, 1792 | **Location:** The salon, then the streets
- The Tuileries assault; monarchy falls
- From the salon, through the ritual mirror-network: every seal tied to royal authority fails simultaneously — Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe is managing multiple failure reports at once; it looks like a dispatch board
- Émilie and Charles watch from the rooftop; the city is transformed; the supernatural transformation runs parallel to the political one but nobody in the streets knows it
- End of the chapter: the count of active practitioners is now half what it was at the novel's opening; this is stated clearly

### Chapter 11: September
**Date:** September 2–7, 1792 | **Location:** Paris streets, the salon
- The September Massacres — mass deaths generate massive plebeian magical energy
- Entities break through in multiple locations simultaneously; coordinated mass manifestation; the first time the Bound Ones have been this close to free in centuries
- Public supernatural incidents cannot be explained away; the official narrative is hysteria and revolutionary fever
- Émilie fighting to hold the salon seal with insufficient practitioners; she discovers she can do the work of two practitioners; she does not understand why; Madame does not explain it yet

### Chapter 12: Afterward
**Date:** October 1792 | **Location:** Salon, Charles's family home
- The quiet after: marriage ceremony (intimate, necessary, not romantic-novel-marriage — this is a working alliance enacted)
- Émilie and Charles living in the new arrangement; learning each other's magical signatures; working together
- Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe's assessment of what remains: she knows the seals will not survive what is coming; she is preparing Émilie for what she does not yet tell her
- Tussaud's chronicle is growing; she knows more than almost anyone; she is now a dangerous person to know

---

## ACT 3: DETERIORATION (1793)
*~12,500 words | Chapters 13–18*
*Stylistic model: Shirley Jackson — quiet dread, the normal world becoming threatening, institutional horror*

**Arc:** The net closes. Every resource is consumed. The last defenses fail.
**Historical anchors:** King's execution January 21, 1793. Terror begins. Foreign Plot prosecution.

---

### Chapter 13: The King's Last Day
**Date:** January 21, 1793 | **Location:** Paris, the salon
- The regicide; from the salon, Madame watches through the ritual mirror what cannot be stopped; the last royal blood-magic connection breaks with the execution
- Specific category of binding fails entirely — the seals that required royal blood authority collapse; the Bound Ones surge through those locations
- What happens to a city when multiple supernatural containment points fail simultaneously and the population does not know it is happening
- Émilie's mark from the Bound Ones reacts for the first time; she does not understand why; the cold that precedes manifestation now comes from inside her — briefly

### Chapter 14: The Terror Begins
**Date:** Spring–Summer 1793 | **Location:** The execution grounds, the salon, Tussaud's studio
- The guillotine creates a specific resonance; mass death + mass witness simultaneously; the most concentrated plebeian energy possible; the execution grounds become supernaturally charged
- Tussaud is forced to take death masks of the executed; she is experiencing what the execution grounds have become; it is leaving marks on her that she documents
- Saint-Just's investigation intensifies; he is now formally empowered by the Committee
- The salon is still operational but barely; fewer ritual gatherings; more emergency responses; Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe is aging visibly under the weight of it

### Chapter 15: Surveillance
**Date:** Summer 1793 | **Location:** The salon, the streets
- The salon under watch; Émilie becomes expert at code-switching — magic discussion compressed to the most oblique language in an instant; this is Madame's training, now fully internalized
- Charles's family under investigation; his family's seal locations are gone; he has nothing to maintain except the salon circle
- First direct approach by Saint-Just's agents — a formal social call that is an intelligence-gathering operation; Émilie and Madame perform perfect normalcy while being investigated
- Tussaud as the outside observer; she documents what she witnesses without being able to intervene

### Chapter 16: The Foreign Plot
**Date:** Autumn 1793 | **Location:** Revolutionary tribunals, the salon
- The Foreign Plot prosecution — Saint-Just has identified the noble ritualists as conspirators; he is right about everything except the nature of what they are conspiring about
- His investigation is shown from the inside; he is intelligent, methodical, ideologically driven, and he has correctly identified something real
- Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe's strategic response: she continues the rituals, continues the defenses, refuses to stop because stopping means the Bound Ones break free long before the judicial timeline catches up to them
- Émilie understanding: Saint-Just's prosecution is, indirectly, the entities' work; he is their unwitting instrument

### Chapter 17: Narrowing
**Date:** Late 1793 | **Location:** Salon, Tussaud's studio
- The practitioner count is now critical; barely enough for a functional ritual circle
- Charles is injured during a manifestation — this is the novel's lowest personal point before the final crisis; he recovers but the recovery reveals how vulnerable they are
- The seals at specific named locations are gone now — the map has collapsed to the salon and a handful of other locations
- Last desperate consultation between Madame, Émilie, and Tussaud — three women who know more than anyone else alive and cannot speak it publicly; what they plan and what they hope

### Chapter 18: The Last Ritual
**Date:** Early 1794 | **Location:** Salon de Sainte-Amaranthe
- Final full ritual attempt; every remaining practitioner gathered
- The ritual fails — not catastrophically, but it is insufficient; the seals are now more hole than seal
- Saint-Just's net is closing; someone in the ritual circle has been reporting
- The chapter ends on suspension — the ritual has failed, the investigation is closing, and the Bound Ones are testing every remaining barrier simultaneously

---

## ACT 4: CULMINATION (June–July 1794)
*~12,500 words | Chapters 19–24*
*Stylistic model: Du Maurier meets Rice — formal restraint giving way to sensory intensity as the boundary collapses*

**Arc:** The last practitioners are arrested, imprisoned, executed. What was supposed to end the story instead transforms it.
**Historical anchor:** The red shirt prisoners — arrested June 1794, executed July 17, 1794.

---

### Chapter 19: Red Shirts
**Date:** June 1794 | **Location:** The salon, then the streets
- The arrests; the red shirt prisoners — the last noble practitioners among them; Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe arrested with them
- Émilie's status: she is not yet arrested; Charles is protecting her; she is watching her mother taken
- The Bound Ones surge visibly as the last practitioners are removed; Émilie is now the only functional practitioner in the salon
- What Émilie realizes: she has been the seal for some time and did not know it; her instinctive power has been doing what the ritual circle was doing; this is terrifying

### Chapter 20: The Cells
**Date:** June–July 1794 | **Location:** Prison (La Conciergerie)
- Émilie gets access to the prison — Charles's contacts, a risk; she visits her mother
- The cells: the practitioners are continuing rituals in prison, improvised, with far less than they need; it is not enough
- Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe begins telling Émilie what she has not told her — the full history; what the mark means; what Émilie actually is; what the ending requires
- The conversation that is the novel's heart: not tender, not clean, not resolution — the truth told in a prison cell under watch, in the coded language they have always used, while the Bound Ones press against everything from every direction

### Chapter 21: The Portrait
**Date:** July 1794 | **Location:** Prison, Tussaud's studio
- Tussaud visits — she is being used by the revolutionary authorities to take death masks; she is in the prison legitimately
- The meeting between Tussaud and Émilie in the prison; the first time they have spoken since Madame's arrest; Tussaud's chronicle has grown into something she knows she cannot show anyone living
- The wax portrait Tussaud made of Émilie in Chapter 4 — Tussaud brings a sketch; the mark from the Bound Ones is visible in the portrait though it cannot be seen on Émilie's skin in normal light
- The mark's significance begins to be revealed; Émilie does not have all the pieces yet; Tussaud does not either; between them they assemble most of it

### Chapter 22: The Night Before
**Date:** July 16, 1794 | **Location:** Prison
- The last night; Madame de Sainte-Amaranthe's final conversation with Émilie
- Not farewell — instruction; Madame tells Émilie what to do with the mark, with the synthesis, with the moment of maximum energy that is coming
- What Madame knows that she has never said: she has always known this is how it ends; she has been preparing Émilie since Chapter 3, not for survival but for the binding
- The conversation is overheard, partially, by Saint-Just — he understands nothing; he writes in his notes that the woman is delusional

### Chapter 23: The Guillotine
**Date:** July 17, 1794 | **Location:** Place du Trône Renversé
- The execution of the red shirt prisoners — 54 people guillotined in 35 minutes; the fastest sequence of the Terror
- Tussaud is present; she is required to be present; her narration carries this chapter; the first time she is direct witness and not rendering Émilie's account
- The energy of 54 simultaneous deaths with an enormous crowd watching — the most concentrated magical event in the novel's history; the Bound Ones are seconds from complete breakthrough
- Émilie's synthesis: her instinctive magic, operating at maximum scale, responds simultaneously to the dying noble practitioners and the surging plebeian energy; she becomes the seal; not by choice exactly but by being what she is in the moment it is needed
- What Tussaud sees and cannot explain; what Saint-Just sees and will never speak of

### Chapter 24: The Chronicle Begins
**Date:** July–December 1794 | **Location:** Tussaud's studio, Paris
- Aftermath; the world does not know what was prevented; the revolution continues; the age of magic is over
- Tussaud sitting with what she witnessed; she cannot tell anyone; Saint-Just cannot speak of it either — she watches him not speak of it, which confirms everything
- Charles: his fate; his silence; what he and Tussaud share
- Tussaud takes out the first sheet of paper; the final lines of the novel are the first lines of her chronicle; the novel we have read is the document she is beginning to write
- The last image: cold has not come since July 17; the Bound Ones are bound; the wax portrait of Émilie sits in the studio; it looks at nothing; it preserves everything
